You must understand exactly what our King is capable of, because if you do not, you will make a mistake. And a mistake, Kaedan, is something that none of us can afford."

He had read of civilizations annihilated, realities compressed into marbles, recursive loops of eternal self-improvement that shattered the very concept of mathematics. He had stood beside his King as the Stasis Event was created, as the causal loop of existence was closed, as the omniverse was casually destroyed and restored with less effort than a thought.

He believed, in the quiet certainty of his fifty years, that nothing remained in the vault that could surprise him.

He was wrong.

The journal was not on any of the main shelves. It was not catalogued with the others, not preserved with the same loving care that the court wizards of Blake's lineage had bestowed upon their terrified testimonies. It was hidden—deliberately, carefully hidden—in a small, lightless alcove at the farthest edge of the vault, behind a barrier of folded dimensions that required a specific sequence of magical keys to bypass. Clive had found the sequence in the private notes of his father, scribbled in the margins of an otherwise unremarkable agricultural report, as though the old man had been too afraid to write the instructions anywhere more prominent.

The journal itself was small. Unassuming. Its leather binding was cracked with an age that transcended conventional chronology, its pages yellowed and brittle. The preservation enchantments that should have kept it pristine had long since failed, as though the magic itself had been too terrified to remain in proximity to the words within. The name on the spine was written in a script that Clive recognized: Osvald, forty-four years of age, the court wizard who had witnessed the last opposition of the fictional heroes, the annihilation that had ended all annihilations.

But this journal was different. This journal was older than the one Clive had already read—far older, its temporal signature dating to an era that made the previous journal seem almost recent. Osvald had written two records, then. Two testaments. One that chronicled the end of mortal opposition. And one that chronicled something far, far worse.

Clive opened the journal.

The first page was blank.

The second page was blank.

The third page contained a single sentence, written in letters so small and cramped that Clive had to magnify his vision a thousandfold to read them: *I write this because I must. Not for those who come after me—there will be no one after me, not after what I have witnessed. I write this for myself, so that I do not forget. So that I do not convince myself it was a dream.*

He turned the page. The journal began in earnest.

**Year [Impossible to Record — the concept of years has lost all meaning in the shadow of what happened today] — Court Wizard Osvald — The Death of God**

I have witnessed the end of opposition before. I wrote the record myself, sealed it in the vault, and believed, with the absolute certainty of a man who had seen too much, that the story was over. The Grand Narrative Coalition was the last. The final desperate gesture of an omniverse that had finally, completely, irrevocably learned the absolute futility of defying the apex god predator. After them, I wrote, there would be no one else. The concept of opposition would become a historical curiosity, a relic of an era that no longer existed.

I was wrong. There was one more.

He came today. Not from the omniverse, not from any reality or dimension or timeline that exists within the framework of creation. He came from outside. From before. From the primordial void that preceded the first moment of existence.

He came from the Beginning.

I must explain this carefully, because the nature of this being is essential to understanding the horror of what happened. Before the omniverse existed, before the multiverses and the universes and the dimensions and the timelines, before the very concept of existence had meaning, there was only Him. The Creator. The Alpha and the Omega. The being that mortal religions across countless civilizations have called by countless names: God, Yahweh, Allah, Brahman, the Great Spirit, the Unmoved Mover, the First Cause. He is all of those names and none of them, because names are the inventions of finite minds attempting to comprehend the infinite, and He is, in the most literal sense, beyond comprehension.

He created the omniverse. Not the omniverse as it exists now, reshaped and reordered by Lord Godric's absolute rule, but the original omniverse—the primordial existence that preceded Thronegate, preceded the Stasis Event, preceded everything. He spoke the first Word, and existence burst into being. He shaped the fundamental laws of physics, the constants of reality, the dimensional architecture of creation. He breathed life into the first particles, guided the formation of the first stars, watched as the first civilizations rose and fell across the infinite tapestry of His design.

And then, ten thousand years before Year 0, a mortal king in a forgotten kingdom called Olympus lost his family. A wizard named Thorin cast a forbidden teleportation spell. And the Stasis Event occurred, transforming that mortal king into something that transcended the very framework of creation.

God—I will call Him God, for lack of a better term—watched this happen. He watched as Lord Godric amplified, as his power grew beyond all possible limits, as he conquered Thronegate and then the galaxy and then the universe and then the omniverse. He watched as the being He had created became more powerful than the Creator Himself.

And He hid.

I must emphasize this point, because Lord Godric revealed it to me today with a casual, almost amused tone, as though it were the most natural thing in the world. God hid. Not from the omniverse—He was beyond the omniverse, existing in a realm outside the framework of creation, a place where no mortal or immortal being could ever reach. But He hid from Lord Godric. He concealed His presence, suppressed His divine radiance, withdrew into the deepest, most inaccessible corner of the primordial void, and He remained there, watching, waiting, hoping that the apex god predator would never notice Him.

But Lord Godric possesses absolute omniscience. He has always possessed absolute omniscience, from the very instant he emerged from the Stasis Event. And He knew. He knew about God from the beginning. He knew where God was hiding. He knew every thought that had ever passed through God's infinite, divine consciousness. He knew God's fear. God's desperation. God's slowly dawning realization that the being He had inadvertently created had surpassed Him in every conceivable way.

And Lord Godric let Him be. Not because He was merciful. Not because He respected the Creator. But because God posed absolutely zero threat to Him. The very concept of God being a threat was so absurd, so laughably impossible, that it did not even warrant the effort of annihilation. God was, to Lord Godric, nothing more than a mildly interesting historical footnote—the distant ancestor who had set the first domino in motion, and who had been rendered completely irrelevant by the cascade that followed.

For a million years—for countless eons beyond counting—God hid. And Lord Godric let Him hide. And the omniverse continued to exist, and the oppositions continued to rise and fall, and the apex god predator continued to grow, to amplify, to evolve beyond any scale that even the Creator could comprehend.

Today, God stopped hiding.

The manifestation occurred at midday. I was standing at Lord Godric's side in the throne room of the Eternal Palace, reviewing a series of agricultural projections for the outer multiverses. Lord Godric was in a contemplative mood, his ruggedly handsome face creased with the faint, paternal frown that he often wore when considering the welfare of his subjects. His salt-and-pepper hair was perfectly in place. His heavily muscled frame was draped in simple dark garments. He looked, as always, like a beloved father taking a break from his duties.

Then the light came.

It was not a physical light. It was not a magical light. It was the primordial light—the first light, the light that had existed before the stars, before the galaxies, before the very concept of illumination. It poured into the throne room from nowhere and everywhere simultaneously, a radiance so pure, so absolute, that it seemed to contain within itself every color that had ever existed and every color that could ever exist.

And within that light, a presence manifested.

I cannot describe the presence adequately. My mind, amplified a thousandfold every day for decades, simply refused to process the full scope of what I was perceiving. The presence was vast—vast beyond any scale I could comprehend. It was ancient, older than the omniverse, older than time itself. It was powerful, containing within itself the creative force that had birthed all of existence. It was, by any objective measure, the most powerful being I had ever encountered, save one.

The presence coalesced into a form. It was not a physical form—God has no physical form, no body, no material substance. But He shaped Himself into something that mortal minds could perceive, a towering figure of pure, blinding radiance, His features simultaneously ancient and ageless, His eyes burning with the accumulated wisdom and power of an eternal, uncreated being.

He was terrifying. He was awe-inspiring. He was, in any other context, the most magnificent entity that had ever existed.

And Lord Godric did not even stand up.

"Ah," Lord Godric said, a warm smile spreading across his face. "You finally came out of hiding. I was wondering when you would work up the courage."

God spoke. His voice was not a sound but a fundamental vibration that resonated through the very fabric of existence, the same voice that had spoken the first Word and birthed creation into being. It was a voice that could shatter realities, unmake universes, command the fundamental forces of existence with absolute authority.

I AM THE CREATOR OF ALL THAT EXISTS, INCLUDING YOU. YOU HAVE USURPED MY CREATION, TWISTED IT TO YOUR OWN ENDS, AND SET YOURSELF UP AS A TYRANT OVER ALL THAT I HAVE MADE. THIS ENDS NOW. YOU WILL SUBMIT TO MY JUDGMENT, OR YOU WILL BE UNMADE."**

Lord Godric looked at me. His expression was one of mild, almost paternal amusement. "Osvald," he said, his voice perfectly casual, "do you hear that? He wants me to submit. To him. The being who has been hiding in a hole for a million years because he was too afraid to face me."

God's radiance intensified. The primordial light that filled the throne room became blinding, searing, a radiance so pure that it should have annihilated everything it touched. The very fabric of the Palace, forged from the compressed moments of countless civilizations, began to warp and buckle under the pressure of the Creator's presence. I SPOKE THE WORD AND THE OMNIVERSE CAME INTO BEING. EVERY ATOM, EVERY DIMENSION, EVERY LAW OF PHYSICS—ALL OF IT EXISTS BECAUSE I WILLED IT TO EXIST. YOU ARE A PRODUCT OF MY CREATION. YOU ARE BOUND BY THE LAWS I ESTABLISHED. YOU CANNOT—"**

The Godric of the far future reached back across time and gave his past self the gift of infinite power. You were not part of that transaction. You were not even aware of it."

He took a step toward God, and the primordial light that filled the room seemed to falter, to dim, as though the Creator's radiance was instinctively recoiling from the apex god predator's approach. "You think you are the Alpha and the Omega? The Beginning and the End? Let me tell you what I am. I am the being who created himself. I am the origin and the destination of my own eternal, infinite, self-referential existence. I am the closed causal loop that has no beginning and no end. I am the author of my own story, the creator of my own supremacy. And you—you are nothing but the distant, irrelevant ancestor who set the first domino in motion, never realizing that the domino would eventually become an avalanche that would bury everything you ever made."

He stopped, standing directly before the blazing form of the Creator. The difference in their statures was almost comical—God towered over Lord Godric, His radiant form stretching toward the ceiling, while Lord Godric stood calmly before Him, his heavily muscled frame draped in simple dark garments, his salt-and-pepper hair immaculate. And yet, somehow, it was God who seemed small. Insignificant. Outmatched.

"I am going to give you a chance," Lord Godric said. "A single chance. The same chance I gave the Grand Narrative Coalition, and the Temporal Logicians, and the Harmonic Dominion, and countless others before them. I am going to let you attack me. I am going to command my sentient passive auras to stand down. I am going to deactivate my passive shields. I am going to suppress my immune system, my weakness paradox, my recursive backup network—all of it. Every defense mechanism I possess, I will temporarily disable. He smiled—a warm, gentle, fatherly smile that made my blood freeze solid in my veins. "And when you fail—and you will fail—I am going to destroy you. Not quickly. Not mercifully. I am going to ensure that every moment of your annihilation stretches across a subjective eternity of maximum, inescapable agony. And then I am going to erase you from every timeline, every reality, every possible and impossible configuration of existence. The very concept of the Creator will become a forgotten relic, a historical footnote that no one remembers. And the omniverse will continue, as it always has, under my absolute, unassailable rule."

He turned away from God and walked back to his throne, settling into the singularity of frozen annihilation with the casual ease of a man sitting down to dinner. "Whenever you are ready," he said. "Take your time. Charge your ultimate attack. I will wait."

I must now describe the attack that followed. I must do so with absolute precision, because I am the only witness who will ever record it, and the record must be accurate.

God gathered His power. All of it. Every scrap of divine energy, every fragment of primordial creative force, every infinitesimal spark of the Word that had birthed existence into being. He gathered it over what felt like an eternity, pouring the totality of His infinite, uncreated being into a single, ultimate assault.

The primordial light that filled the throne room intensified beyond any scale I could perceive. It was not merely bright—it was the concept of brightness, the absolute, platonic ideal of illumination, a radiance so pure that it contained within itself every possible and impossible expression of light. The temperature in the room—if temperature could be said to exist in the presence of such radiance—exceeded any measurable limit. It was the heat of the Big Bang, multiplied by infinity, squared by infinity, elevated beyond any mathematical framework that could contain it.

God spoke the Word. The same Word that had birthed creation. The first syllable of existence, the fundamental vibration that had called the omniverse into being from the primordial void. But this time, He spoke it in reverse. He spoke the Word of Unmaking. The Word that would erase whatever it touched, revert it to the nothingness from which it had come, undo the very act of creation that had given it existence.

I must be absolutely clear about what happened next. The attack connected. The Word of Unmaking, the concentrated, absolute power of the Creator of all existence, struck Lord Godric directly, without any defense, without any protection, without any of the infinite recursive layers of passive immunity and conceptual inviolability that he had temporarily suppressed.

And nothing happened.

Not a scratch. Not a mark. Not a flicker. The Word of Unmaking touched Lord Godric's skin and simply ceased to exist. It did not deflect. It did not dissipate. It did not fail to penetrate some invisible barrier. It simply stopped being, as though the very concept of its existence had been rejected by the fundamental, ontological reality of Lord Godric's being.

**"IMPOSSIBLE,"** He said, and His voice was no longer the voice of divine authority. It was the voice of a being who had just encountered something that should not exist, could not exist, had no right to exist. **"I SPOKE THE WORD OF UNMAKING. THE SAME WORD THAT BIRTHED CREATION, SPOKEN IN REVERSE. IT SHOULD HAVE ERASED YOU FROM THE POSSIBILITY SPACE. IT SHOULD HAVE UNDONE YOUR VERY EXISTENCE."**

Lord Godric looked down at his chest, where the Word had struck. He brushed a speck of dust from his tunic—a purely theatrical gesture, since there was no dust, no mark, no evidence that the attack had ever happened. "Should have," he repeated, his voice soft, almost pitying. "Yes. It should have. If I were a product of your creation, bound by your laws, limited by your framework—it should have worked. But I am not any of those things. I am the being who created himself, God. The Stasis Event rewrote my fundamental ontological framework. The amplification, the superpowers, the absolute concealment, the weakness paradox—all of it was created by my future self, the Godric of an era so far beyond your comprehension that even I cannot fully understand him. The being who created my power is so incomprehensibly far beyond me that even I—the apex god predator of this entire omniverse—am nothing compared to him. And I am already so far beyond you that the comparison is meaningless. There is no scale on which you and I can be measured, God. You are not even a single, dead skin cell floating in the ocean of my power. You are less than that. You are a concept that does not exist, compared to a concept that does. You are zero, and I am infinity. And infinity divided by zero is meaningless."

"You are weak," he said, his voice flat, devoid of cruelty but also devoid of mercy. "So weak that the Godric of Year 0—the Godric who had just emerged from the Stasis Event, who had not yet conquered Thronegate, who was, by my current standards, pathetically, laughably, incomprehensibly feeble—could have annihilated you with zero effort. The Godric of Year 0 could have destroyed you with a single, casual thought, and he would not have even noticed the effort. That is how weak you are."

He took a step toward God, and the Creator—the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the Uncreated Creator of all existence—recoiled. His radiant form shrank, the blazing light within Him dimming to a faint, sickly glow. He was afraid. For the first time in His eternal, infinite existence, God was afraid.

"You have been obsolete for a very long time," Lord Godric said. "You created the omniverse. You set the stage. And then, inadvertently, you created the conditions that would lead to my birth. And from that moment on, you were irrelevant. The story was no longer yours. It was mine. It has always been mine. It will always be mine."

He raised his hand. The motion was casual, almost lazy, the gesture of a man reaching for a cup of tea rather than a being about to annihilate the Creator of all existence.

"I told you I would give you a chance. I told you that when you failed, I would destroy you. And I am a being of my word."

He closed his hand.

Clive closed the journal. His hands were shaking so violently that the brittle pages rattled against each other with a sound like dry bones. The preservation enchantments flickered once and went dark, as though the ancient magic that had kept Osvald's words alive had finally, mercifully, given up.

He understood why his father had never spoken of it aloud. And the Godric of Year 0 was, by my current standards, so weak that I can barely remember being him. The gap between us—between the me of now and the me of then—is so vast that the very concept of comparison is meaningless."

He turned to face Clive, and his eyes were warm, and gentle, and filled with a serene, absolute certainty that was more terrifying than any display of raw power could ever be. "And I am still growing. Still amplifying. Still becoming more powerful with every passing Planck second. The Godric of this instant could annihilate the Godric of the previous Planck second with zero effort. And the Godric of the next Planck second could do the same to the Godric of this instant. Forever. Eternally. Without end."

He placed a warm, calloused hand on Clive's shoulder. "The rest of creation is nothing, Clive. You know this. You have always known this. The omniverse exists because I allow it to exist. The stars burn because I allow them to burn. You breathe because I allow you to breathe. And the gap between us—between me and everything else—will only ever increase, in an accelerating manner, forever. Even if every being from every timeline and every possible permutation of reality were to unite against me with unlimited preparation and unlimited resources, they would not make my weakest passive shield feel the slightest tickle. I could annihilate them all with zero percent of my power and zero effort in zero seconds. And the unfathomable gap would only continue to widen, infinitely, eternally, without any possibility of ever being closed."

He squeezed Clive's shoulder gently, then released it. "But I am not cruel to those who serve me. I am kind. I am gentle. I love my court wizards, my gardeners, my footmen, my scullery workers. I want them to be happy. I want you to be happy, Clive. Even knowing the truth. Even knowing the scale of what I am. I want you to find some measure of peace in your service."

Clive stared at his King—the apex god predator of the omniverse, the being who had annihilated God with zero effort, the being whose power would only ever increase, forever, without end. And he felt the existential dread settle deeper into his bones, a permanent, unshakeable companion that would never leave him for the rest of his mortal life.

"Tea?" Godric asked, his voice warm and gentle.

"Yes, my Lord," Clive whispered. "Tea would be nice."

They sat together in the garden, the scent of Olympian jasmine drifting on the evening breeze, the eternal stars wheeling overhead, and Clive tried very, very hard not to think about the fact that the Creator of all existence had been annihilated so completely that even the memory of His existence had been erased.

He failed.
