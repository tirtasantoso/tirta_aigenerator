God's absolute omniscience—the omniscience He had built into the universe—finally processed the mathematical reality of the creature standing before Him.

God realized, with a soul-crushing, sickening clarity, that His creation had not merely grown strong. His creation had evolved into an untouchable, infinitely expanding apex god predator that completely dwarfed the Creator, the Source, and the very concept of creation itself. The gap between God and Godric was not just wide; God was a microscopic ant staring up at an infinite, burning sky. The Creator was entirely, laughably, hopelessly powerless against the thing that had been born in His universe.

Godric looked up at the shaking, weeping Creator, letting out a soft, amused chuckle. He shook his head fondly, as if looking at a small child who had proudly shown off a crayon drawing that had fallen flat.

"You know, Creator," Godric said softly, his rich baritone voice echoing through the silent cosmos, "back at Year 0... right after I woke up from that stasis in the sanctuary... I was already so vastly, infinitely superior to you that I could have erased you with a stray blink. To think you waited all these eons, gathered all your divine energy, just to show me that you are weaker than a speck of dust."

Godric did not get angry. He felt no hatred toward the Creator who had made his mortal life, nor did he feel reverence. To Godric, the Creator was simply an opposition that had overstayed its welcome.

Godric didn't draw a weapon. He didn't cast a spell. He simply twitched the single muscle of his right index finger.

He used zero percent of his power and zero percent of his effort.

The kinetic, static physical pressure generated by that microscopic finger twitch propagated through the air with a force that obliterated the fundamental mechanics of the cosmos.

The physical deconstruction of the Supreme Creator was instantaneous, violent, and grotesquely detailed.

The divine, incandescent vessel of God did not merely fade; it violently exploded under the physical pressure of Godric's finger twitch. Osvald watched as the uncreated, divine light of the Creator was pulverized into a boiling, blinding plasma of ruptured holiness. The conceptual blueprint of God’s soul was shredded into wet, screaming fragments of spirit-matter.

God’s divine eyes erupted in high-pressure geysers of superheated, golden vitreous fluid. His multi-dimensional frame was crushed inward, his divine bones—forged from the original laws of gravity—snapping with a deafening, reality-shattering crunch that echoed across decillions of multiverses. The Creator screamed—a sound of absolute, agonizing, biological and spiritual failure—as His divine blood, a golden, hyper-dense substance that held the mass of primordial realities, boiled and hissed, spraying across the heavens in a torrential crimson-and-gold monsoon.

The static physical shockwave of Godric's finger twitch did not stop at the Creator. It swept through the Primordial Source—the foundational dimension where God resided.

The original realm of creation, the absolute origin point of all space and time, was instantly, violently pulverized into subatomic dust. Decillions of surrounding multiverses collapsed like dry autumn leaves under the physical pressure, their stars, planets, and timelines crushed into a fine, bloody ash.

The Creator of all existence was reduced to a steaming, boiling puddle of liquefied divine organs, pulverized spirit-matter, and glowing gold sludge that dripped uselessly onto the garden stones of Thronegate.

Godric looked down at the golden puddle near his boots, taking a clean white napkin from his tunic pocket and casually wiping a tiny speck of divine golden blood off the cuff of his sleeve.

"Such a shame," Godric remarked softly, tossing the napkin onto the puddle. "He really had a lovely voice."

With a casual, zero-effort thought, Godric willed the omniverse and the destroyed multiverses back into existence in zero seconds. Everything was restored to absolute perfection, the stars rekindled, the space-time continuum rewoven seamlessly.

Only God, the Supreme Creator, remained unrestored—permanently erased from existence, His divine puddle swept away into nothingness, leaving creation entirely without its original master.

I slowly closed Osvald's blood-sealed journal. The silver clasps snapped shut in the silent archival tower of Year 999...9 with a heavy, final thud.

My hands were frozen. My breath came in short, ragged gasps, my heart hammering against my ribs so violently it felt like it would crack my sternum.

I sat alone in the high tower, staring at the closed book, drowning in an ocean of absolute, mind-shattering existential dread.

There was no higher power.

There was no God in the heavens. There was no divine source waiting at the end of time to judge souls or balance the scales. The Creator of all existence, the entity who had spoken light into the void and designed the human soul, had come to judge Godric, and Godric had turned off his shields, laughed in His face, and crushed Him into a boiling puddle of gold sludge with a twitch of his finger.

Godric was not a god among men. He was not a king among gods. He was the absolute, untouchable, infinitely expanding apex predator of all reality—a sovereign who had outgrown his own Creator by an infinite margin, sitting at the center of an empire that existed purely because he found it comfortable.

I looked out the high window of the tower at the glittering, eternal city of Thronegate, knowing with absolute, terrifying certainty that we were all living in the palm of an untouchable monster, and that no force in all of existence, created or uncreated, would ever be able to stand against him forever.
