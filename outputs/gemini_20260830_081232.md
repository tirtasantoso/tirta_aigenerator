The universe itself will break its own laws, tear its own flesh, and rewrite its own history before it allows our King to be inconvenienced."

Kaedan stared at the bound volumes resting on the stone shelves—hundreds of journals, written across a million years by his ancestors, each one a monument to absolute futility. He understood now why his predecessors had written them. It was not for historical record; it was the desperate, necessary act of mortal minds trying to anchor themselves to sanity after witnessing the total, horrific lack of challenge that defined their master’s existence.

He understood, with absolute, terrifying clarity, that Godric was not merely an unbeatable sovereign. He was the undisputed, eternal apex predator of creation, an entity who could completely annihilate the entire omniverse an infinite number of times in zero seconds with zero effort, sitting upon his throne with a warm, genuine, fatherly smile, waiting for his beloved court wizards to come join him for tea.

Kaedan took a deep, shuddering breath, wiped the cold sweat from his brow, and adjusted the ceremonial robes of his office. The existential dread was heavy in his marrow, an eternal, freezing weight that would never leave him. Yet, beneath the horror, the inherited bloodline of Blake stirred within his chest, filling him with a fierce, terrified, and unyielding loyalty.

"Come, Kaedan," Corin said gently, turning toward the heavy iron doors of the archive. "Lord Godric has just returned from the eastern gardens. He asked if his new court wizard would care to join him for the afternoon walk."

Kaedan nodded, swallowed the dry knot of terror in his throat, and followed his father up the stone stairs, walking directly into the light of the smiling, absolute god who held totality in the palm of his resting hand.
