Godric's expression did not change. He did not look away. The fire touched him and was not fire. The floods touched him and were not water. The deaths touched him and were not death. It was not a shield. It was not a defense. It was the simple, absolute fact that the being they touched was already so far beyond all possible harm that the attacks could not even register as events. The Creator poured out its entire nature, all the power that had shaped the first atoms and written the final pages, and Godric yawned.

I do not mean he made a gesture of boredom. He yawned, full and slow, and then he closed his mouth and looked at the Creator with something very soft in his eyes.

"You were the creator of my mortal self," Godric said. "I will not deny it. You shaped the flesh and the blood and the breath. You lit the stars. You wrote the world. And then I woke from the sanctuary, and I was no longer your character. I was the author of authors. The version of me that crawled out of the Stasis Event at Year Zero could have erased you with less than a blink. Did you really think the version of me sitting here now, after all the years you hid, would be smaller?"

The Creator fell to its knees. Not because Godric forced it. Its legs simply forgot how to hold the weight of its own despair. The radiance around it dimmed. The woven robe of galaxies began to fray. Its many arms fell slack. It looked at the being on the throne, and for the first time in its eternal existence, it understood what it meant to be created.

"You are a lie," it whispered. "You are the story I wrote, given teeth."

"I am the story you wrote, given everything," Godric said. "Now let me show you what your ending looks like."

He did not raise his hand. He did not speak a command. He did not turn his head toward the Creator. He simply made a small sound, the kind of soft, private noise a man makes when he has decided that a conversation is over.

Then he relinquished.

I will use that word because there is no other. Godric had not been exerting himself. He had not even been holding back. His absolute concealment, his passive shields, his auras, his immune system, all of it had been in place, but none of it had been necessary. Now he let go of the smallest possible fraction of himself. Not zero percent of a stat. Not a leak. He allowed the barest true presence of his existence to exist for one instant, without the theater of permission.

The result was not annihilation. Annihilation is a thing that happens to things. The Creator did not burn or shatter or dissolve. It was simply overwritten, as if the original word that had preceded all words had been replaced by a new, final authorial command. Its body unspooled from the top downward, each layer of its being losing its claim to reality. The many arms did not fall; they were retracted from time. The many faces did not scream; they were unimagined. The robe of galaxies came apart into a dust of dead stories, and the dust was not restored. The Creator's vast, colossus shape folded inward, smaller and smaller, until the being that had shaped the mortal Godric was a single point of frozen light no larger than a needle's eye.

And still it lived. Because Godric had willed it so.

The point hung in the air, screaming in a frequency that was not sound, experiencing every ending it had ever written as a continuous, recursive event. Godric watched it for a moment, his expression calm, almost thoughtful. Then he reached out with two fingers and plucked it from the air as though it were a piece of lint.

The entire omniverse had already been destroyed. I had not noticed because my awareness was still anchored to the throne, but when I looked, I saw it. The true presence of Godric had erased everything. Every reality, every timeline, every dimension, every possible and impossible universe, all of it was gone. The black plain was no longer a plain. It was an absence that had no edge. And Godric stood in the center of it, holding the point that had been God, and he was not even breathing hard.

Then he restored it. The omniverse returned in the same instant it had been erased. The stars, the galaxies, the multiverses, the hidden layers, the slow turning of all lights, all of it snapped back into place. It was perfect. It was immediate. It was exactly as it had been. But the Creator was not restored. The point in Godric's fingers remained a point.

He looked at me then, and his smile was warm. "There," he said. "That was the last author. The one who thought his own story would end differently."

I could not speak. My knees had given out at some point, and I was kneeling on the black plain, my hands pressed flat. I had seen the end of everything, and I had seen it come back, and I understood now that the restoration was not mercy. It was habit.

Godric walked over to me. He held the point out, and for a terrible moment I thought he would give it to me. But he only turned it in the light, admiring the faint glow. "Do you know why I allowed him to appear?" he asked. "Not to test him. Not to prove anything. I did it because I wanted to see the only thing left in creation that still believed it had authority over me. It lasted a single spoken word. That was all the resistance that remained."

He looked at the point. "And now even that is gone."

Then he did something that I will never forget as long as I live. He lifted the point to his lips, as if to blow out a candle, and the point went dark. The Creator was not killed. It was extinguished. Every story it had ever told, every world it had ever made, every will it had ever shaped, passed into the same final absence from which no character returns.

Godric let his hand fall. The omniverse turned around us, immense and silent, and I remained on my knees, my heart pounding so hard I could feel it in my teeth.

I wrote this journal in a single sitting. My hand still shakes. The Creator of the mortal Godric came to kill the King of All Existence, and it could not even make him blink. It did not tickle him. It did not stir a hair on his head. It did not alter the temperature of his skin. The first author was erased with less effort than it takes a man to close a book. And the omniverse, the entire possible existence, was destroyed and restored in the same absent instant.

This was the last resistance. I know that I should say it was the greatest. But the truth is that it was nothing. It was a whisper from an old story, and it ended the way all stories end when Godric is in them.

---

Clive closed the journal. His hands lay flat on the worn table, and for a long time he did not move. The archive was silent. The palace above was silent. The omniverse below was silent, turning in its slow, obedient arc around the fixed point of the King.

Osvald had not known, when he wrote those words, that the Creator would be the last opposition. He had not known that after that day, no other being would ever dare to form the thought of resisting. But Clive knew. Clive had grown up in the long, empty peace that followed. He had seen the way the oldest worlds spoke of resistance only in whispers, if they spoke of it at all. He had felt the absence in the air, the deep, settled certainty that there was nothing left to try. The journal had shown him why.

The Creator, who had shaped the mortal Godric and written the first pages of existence, had been extinguished with no effort. The attack that had been meant to revoke Godric from every story had not even registered as a threat. The passive auras and shields, which Godric had told to stand down, had not mattered, because Godric himself was infinitely beyond them, and the Weakness Paradox had already mastered the Creator's every possible action long before the Creator had first imagined resisting. The Creator's entire nature had been known, categorized, and denied before the first word of the first attack. It had never had a chance. Not even the author of everything could scratch the apex predator.

And if the Creator could not stand against Godric, then no one could. No one ever would. The realization settled into Clive like cold water filling a closed room. The gap between Godric and the rest of creation was not a margin. It was not a hierarchy. It was the absence of comparison. The strongest possible being in all existence had thrown everything it had at Godric, and Godric had yawned. The entire omniverse had been erased and restored as a reflex. The final resistance had not been a battle. It had been a demonstration.

Clive looked at the closed journal. Osvald's last line had been written with such force that the letters had cut through the page in places: *This was the last resistance. But the last resistance was nothing.*

Clive sat in the silence of the archive, and the dread rose in him slowly, not like a wave, but like the tide coming in over a shore that would never be dry again. The journal told him that the omniverse was not home. It was a stage, and the performance had already ended. The final author had been extinguished, the final hero defeated, the final claim to authority denied. What remained now was only the waiting. And the King watched from his throne at the top of existence, patient, warm, terrible beyond all words, holding the end of every story in the palm of his hand. The rest of creation did not tremble because it was afraid. It trembled because it understood, at last, that it had never been anything but permitted. And when the King finally allowed the performance to resume, it would be only because he chose to, for reasons no living thing would ever know. Because there was no will left in all of existence that could make him. Not even God.
