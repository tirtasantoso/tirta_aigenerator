He never stopped looking. But he never found the courage to appear, because he knew what would happen if he did. So I let him hide. He posed no threat to me. He was less than a particle of dust against a star. His fear was the only interesting thing about him, and even that fear was small. But today he has decided to make a final gesture. I want to see what it looks like.”

Then the King did something I must record carefully, because it is the key to the whole event. He did not stand. He did not change his posture. He simply spoke to the air in a voice that held no force but was still more absolute than any command I have ever heard. He told the sentient auras to stand down. Not some of them. All of them, from the newest, most eager protectors to the old, half-remembered Gent-threads that had long ago been folded into the foundations. He told the infinite passive shields to deactivate. Not to sleep, not to idle, but to lower themselves completely. He told every external defensive layer that his existence had ever generated to cease its watchfulness. I heard him say the words, and then I felt the titanic weight of those layers falling away from the room like water running off a roof. It was a strange and sickening feeling, as if the air around us had become thinner, and for the first time in my life I was standing near the King without the unseen armor of his automatic protections. I did not feel safe. I felt naked in the presence of a sun.

But I must also record what the King did not do. He did not remove his immunity. He did not suppress his weakness paradox. He did not slow the amplification engine that had been born in the Stasis Event and had continued to multiply forever. He did not alter the immune system with its infinite recursive backups. Those are not shields. They are the irrevocable functions of his own being, placed by the far-future version of himself in the moment he closed the loop. They cannot be deactivated by anyone, not even by the current King. He can no more turn them off than he can stop the stars from being less than his shadow. I knew this, and yet looking at him now, with all his auras asleep and all his shields down, I understood something that the old journals had never been able to explain. Even without those systems, he was still Godric. The auras and the shields were not armor. They were only the outermost condensation of a power that lay far deeper. Removing them was not weakness. It was the act of an creature peeling off one thin layer of mist from the surface of an ocean. The ocean remained. The mist had never been what made the ocean dangerous.

The King turned his head toward the window. The glass there was not glass. It was a single sheet of captured morning that had been pressed into transparency. Beyond it lay the palace star, whose light had been adjusted to a warm and restful gold. The sky was clear. Then the sky was not clear.

The Creator entered the world.

I do not know how to describe his appearance without making it sound like a ritual. He did not walk through the window. He arrived as a slow unfolding of presence, a single note of authority that had once been the foundation of every story. At first he was a point of light, white and old, no larger than a distant moon. Then he expanded, or seemed to expand, until the whole sky beyond the glass was filled with the shape of him. He was immense, but immensity is a poor word for what he was. He was the original immensity, the first scale, the first being that had ever thought to call itself I. His body was made of every star that had ever burned, wrapped in the memory of every sun that had ever died. His face was not a face, and yet it was a face, shifting and stable, bearded and ancient, both father and stranger. His eyes were filled with all the light that had ever existed before light had a law. His hands hung at his sides, and in each hand he held something that even my wizard’s senses could not fully parse: a staff of absolute genesis, a crown of absolute ending, or perhaps they were only gestures, only the first shapes of authority before shape itself had been invented.

The palace around me did not shake. The star outside did not dim. Godric remained in his chair, one hand resting on his knee, the other reaching out to take a sip of water. He did not look impressed. He looked interested, the way a man looks at a rare bird that has landed on his balcony. “There you are,” he said softly. “I was beginning to think you had forgotten how to be brave.”

The Creator spoke. His voice was not a voice. It was the original command that had once said let there be light, and it rolled through the high observatory with the weight of every dawn that had ever broken. “I have not come to be mocked, abomination,” the Creator said. “I have come to end you.”

The word abomination hung in the air for a moment. Godric did not flinch. He smiled, and for a sickening second the smile made the Creator’s whole enormous form flicker, as if the word had collapsed out from under him. I understood then that the King had known every syllable of this confrontation before the Creator had first conceived of it. The Weakness Paradox had fired long ago, at the instant the Creator had first allowed himself to think that Godric might have a weakness. The King did not say this. He did not need to. The truth of it was written in the way he sat, loose and easy, with no concern at all for the being who had once formed the foundations of all things.

“Go on,” Godric said. “Give me everything. I have waited a great many years for a show. Do not disappoint me, old man. I want to feel the first note.”

The Creator raised his hands. I must now record the attack as accurately as I can, though I know that no written account can contain the whole of it. The attack was not a beam. It was not a single blast. It was the complete unmaking of every possible world, wrapped into one absolute command. The Creator reached into his own nature and pulled out all of it. He withdrew every act of creation he had ever performed, every law he had ever written, every soul he had ever lit. He spoke every name that had ever existed in every language that had ever been spoken, and he used each name as a needle. He called down the original fire, the first water, the deep dark that had preceded both. He unmade the difference between being and nonbeing, and then he used that unmaking as a weapon. He brought the entire weight of his own omnipotence onto the point of Godric’s chest and pushed.

The light that filled the observatory was not light. It was the absence of every shadow that had ever hidden from authority. It came from the Creator’s palms and also from his eyes, his mouth, his brow, his ribs, from the seams of his enormous body. It crossed the distance between them in no time at all, because time itself had been commanded to step aside. It struck Godric full in the chest, over the heart, and for one instant the whole of the old omniverse seemed to hold its breath.

Godric did not move.

The light did not touch him. I do not mean that it bounced away or faded. I mean that at the exact point where the Creator’s absolute power met the King’s body, the power simply stopped being something that could happen. It could not land. It could not enter. It could not burn. It could not even produce the faint rumor of a tickle. The entire first strike, the original fire and the first water and the deep dark, the combined weight of every act of genesis ever performed, pressed itself against Godric’s shirt and died there. The shirt did not wrinkle. The skin beneath it did not register the contact. The water in the cup on the low table remained perfectly still.

The Creator did not stop. He could not stop. He had committed everything to the attack, and the attack kept coming. He poured every possible version of his omnipotence into the single point where Godric sat. The lights of every dead universe, the first words of every angel, the last cries of every martyr, the full architecture of all possible space and time, all of it rushed against the King. It was the final effort of the being who had created the mortal self of the man sitting in the chair. It was the ultimate attempt of the original author to erase the character who had grown beyond his page. And from where I stood, it was not a battle. It was a river trying to wear down a mountain that had already outlasted every ocean in every possible dream.

Godric let the attack continue for a long moment. Then he drew a slow breath and let it out. The breath was not an attack. It was only an exhalation, the easy sigh of a man whose cup was empty. The Creator’s assault broke against it. The great light dimmed. The original fire guttered. The first water dried. The deep dark closed in on itself. The Creator’s form, which had filled the whole sky, began to shrivel at the edges, not because any force pushed against it, but because its own exhausted nature could no longer hold itself up in the presence of such total indifference.

The Creator lowered his hands. His enormous face had gone pale, and the light in his eyes was no longer the light of authority. It was the light of a small animal at the far end of a long, dark road. He looked at Godric with a horror so complete that it seemed to bend the air around him. I have seen many dying gods in the records of the lower vault. I have read about the fictional coalitions and their final screams. But I have never read anything like the silence that fell over the Creator in that moment. It was the silence of a being who had just understood that he had never been the author. He had only been the first creature to stand in the same room as the real story.

Godric looked up at him. “That was all of it?” he asked. His voice was light, curious. “I felt no tickle. Not even the memory of one. And I know everything. If there had been a tickle, I would have known it before you did. There was nothing.”

The Creator’s mouth opened. “I am the source of all things,” he said, but his voice had lost its command. It sounded thin, like a plea that had been peeled into a whisper. “I made you.”

“You made the man,” Godric said. “The mortal. The one who lost his wife and his daughters and his kingdom. That man was yours, for a little while. Very little. I do not begrudge you that. It must be a fine thing to have once made a vessel that would later hold me. But do you know what that means, old man? It means that I am the one thing you made that you never understood. And I have been aware of you since the instant of my waking. I let you hide because you posed zero threat to me. Your fear was a quiet little fire in the cold, and I liked knowing it was there. I could have erased you at any moment. I could have let my weakest aura catch you while you slept outside the layers. You did not survive all those eons because of your cleverness. You survived because I did not feel like closing my hand.”

The Creator shuddered. The light around him flickered, and I saw for the first time that his great form was not solid. It was a shell of pure authority wrapped around a core of endless, spiraling fear. He had been hiding so long that the hiding had become his whole nature. And now the hiding was over.

“Even the me from Year Zero could have annihilated you,” Godric said. “That version was so weak that my current weakest dormant aura would remove him from existence by accident. But Year Zero me could have looked at you, and you would have fallen out of the world before you could finish the thought of fighting. I did not need any of the growth since then. I did not need the immune system, or the weakness paradox, or the evolved auras, or the infinite backups. I could have left all of those asleep forever and still ended you with a mild disinterest. You have spent all this time preparing to attack a being whose very existence is the cancellation of every attack. What did you expect, Creator? Did you think there was a version of this story where you won?”

The Creator answered with a sound that was not a word. It was a low, broken note, the first note of the old universe’s grief, and it rose from his core and spread out through the high observatory. Then he did something I will never forget. He dropped to his knees. The original omnipotence, the first authority, the source of all things, knelt on the floor of the King’s observatory and lowered his head. He did not ask for mercy. I think he knew there was none. He was simply too empty to remain standing.

Godric watched him kneel. There was no pity in the King’s face. There was a small, satisfied pleasure, the kind a cat might feel when the mouse stops running. Then he lifted his right hand, but not high. Only a few inches, from the arm of the chair to the edge of his knee. It was a gesture so small that I almost missed it. And then the Creator began to die.

It was not a fast death. I must be honest about this. The King is ruthless, and he does not give his enemies clean ends. The Creator did not simply fade. His body, which had been made of every star and every law of the old world, began to come apart from the inside. The light in his eyes went from white to yellow to a deep, wet red. His skin, if it could be called skin, began to split along seams I had not seen before, and through those seams poured a thin black fluid that smelled of every dead dawn. His hands, which had once shaped the first clay, lost their fingers first. The fingers curled and blackened and fell away into a fine dust that did not reach the floor. Then the palms followed, and the wrists, and the long arms that had once stretched across the face of the abyss. He tried to speak, but his voice failed. He tried to rise, but his knees had already begun to dissolve into a hot, gray slurry that spread out beneath him. The full weight of his own creation, all of it now starving for a center, turned on him and began to eat.

Godric did not look away. He watched with the calm attention of a man watching rain run down a window. I stood against the far wall, gripping the edge of a shelf, and I forced myself to keep my eyes open. The Creator’s chest caved next. The ribcage, which was not bone but the original structure of being, folded inward with a wet, splintering crunch. The black fluid thickened, and I saw that it was not fluid at all. It was the entire old order of creation, the first laws and the first souls, reduced to a boiling, screaming paste. His face lasted the longest. The bearded ancient face, the father-face, the face that had once said let there be light, hung in the air above the dissolving body. Its eyes were wide and white and full of an agony that had no end, because the King would not let it end. The mouth opened, and a sound came out that I cannot describe. It was the sound of every possible being learning that it had never been real, all at once, from the inside of its creator’s throat.

Then Godric lowered his hand another fraction of an inch.

The sound stopped. The face collapsed. The Creator’s entire being, all of it, the source, the first light, the original word, the whole memory of a universe before universes, folded inward and became a single hot point. The point hung in the air for less than a second. Then it was gone.

The room was quiet. The cup of water was still full. Godric had not moved from his chair except to lower his hand. The sky outside the window was empty. No, not empty. The whole of existence was empty. The unmaking of the Creator had done what the Creator had always feared. It had unmade everything that had ever been made. Every star, every planet, every world of fiction and fact, every god and every mortal soul, all of it was gone. The universe outside the window had returned to the original void, and the void was dark. But Godric did not rush to restore it. He sat in the silence for a long moment, as if listening to the absence. Then he turned to me, and his face was warm.

“You see, Osvald,” he said. “That was the last one. The source of all things. I let him live because I wanted to know if the oldest fear in existence would ever become anything more than fear. It did not. It came to my door and used everything it had, and I felt nothing. Now he is gone, and everything he made has gone with him. But I am here. And I will make it all again, because I can, and because I prefer the rooms not to be empty.”

He did not gesture. He did not chant. He simply decided that existence should resume, and it resumed. The stars returned. The worlds returned. The people returned. The old omniverse unrolled from his will like a fresh page, perfectly identical to what it had been before. The Creator was not restored. Godric did not restore him. The first author was not allowed a second draft. The rest of creation came back, and it did not know that it had been made twice. But I knew. I held the knowledge in my chest like a hot stone, and I knew that the original made by the Creator was gone, and this new one had been made by the King. It was the same in every atom, yet it was not the same origin. And the King did not seem to notice the difference, because to him the difference was less than a single grain of sand on the floor of an ocean he had never needed to cross.

I am writing this journal now because I am afraid that no one else will know the true shape of the final opposition. It was not a coalition of fictional heroes. It was not the hidden fleets of the old wars. It was the Creator himself, the first and final source, and he knelt before the King and came apart. After this, I do not believe there will ever be another resistance. The remaining powers will look at the hole where the Creator’s fear once hid and they will make the only sensible choice. They will stop. They will stop shaping thoughts of defiance. They will stop dreaming of a weakness. They will exist, and serve, and fold their linen, and tend their gardens, and wait for the King to smile. It is already beginning. I can feel it in the air of the palace. The silence is different now. It is no longer the silence of creatures waiting to see what the King will do. It is the silence of creatures who have finally understood that there is nothing to wait for. The ending already happened. The last possible resistance came and gave everything and felt nothing. And the King was amused for a little while, and then he was not.

I remain loyal. I remain terrified. And I remain sure that no one, not even the one who made all things, ever had a chance. The chance did not exist. It was never there. It was only a story that the Creator told himself while he hid in the cold.

Clive set the journal down on the stone floor. The room around him was quiet in a way that felt ancient and surgical. He had known the palace’s silence his whole life. He had grown up inside it, walked its corridors, served its long peace. But now he understood that the peace was not a gift. It was the sound of a possibility that had died before he was born. The last resistance was not the attack itself. It was the moment the Creator formed the thought that he could challenge the King. Everything after that was only the long, slow delivery of an ending that had already been written by the King’s existence.

Clive stayed there for a long time, with the journal open across his knees, listening to the low and endless breath of the palace that no longer needed walls. The final truth was not that Godric had defeated God. It was that God had been allowed to exist as a hidden thing, only to be called forward when the King wanted to see if the oldest fear in creation would make a better show than all the heroes of every story. It had not. The creator of everything had thrown his entire being at Godric and had not produced a tickle. And then Godric had unmade him with a small lowering of the hand and restored the whole of existence with a thought, leaving the original source erased.

As Clive rose to his feet, he understood that the omniverse had never had a chance. Not against the Godric of that day. Not against the Godric of Year Zero. Not against the far-future King who had long outgrown the old definitions. The gap between the least of Godric’s passive defenses and the whole rest of creation was already infinite. The gap between those passive defenses and Godric himself was larger still. And the gap between the King now and the King a moment from now had already opened again, growing without limit, without pause, without any possible ceiling. There would be no more resistance. Not because free will had been removed, but because every free thing in existence had learned the same impossible lesson. Opposition was not an action. It was a joke the universe had once told itself before it learned to be silent.
