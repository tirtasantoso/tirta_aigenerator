“That was everything?” He asked. “Truly? I felt nothing. Not a tickle. Not a warmth. Not even the faint change of air on my throat. I could have stood here from the beginning and let you try until the end of all time, and you would never have made me blink.”

The Creator did not answer. His fire had gone out. His six wings had folded against His back like the wings of a stunned bird. His halos stopped rotating and hung still in the air, tilting as if they could not remember their purpose. I saw, in the blank face of the First Author, the exact moment when absolute existential dread entered Him. It did not come like a knife. It came like a room with no floor.

His Majesty laughed. Not a cruel laugh. A light, warm chuckle, full of genuine amusement. “You made me mortal once,” He said. “You shaped a soul and called it Godric, and you placed it in a little kingdom on a little world, and you let it love and bleed and fail. And then I woke from the Stasis Event in Year Zero, and I destroyed the strongest galaxy in the old creation without lifting a finger. That version of me, the one just out of stasis, could have erased you from existence with a glance. You were already nothing to him. You are less than nothing to me. Do you understand?”

The Creator’s voice came out as a thin, dry rasp, a sound like the last page of the first book tearing. “I understand,” He said. “You are my work, but you are beyond all works. I am not here to end you. I am here to be ended.”

Godric’s expression softened, but only a little. “Yes,” He said. “You are.”

Then He ended it. But He did so slowly, in the way He might put out a candle with a breath. He leaned forward and looked at the Creator with the full, unmediated attention of His gaze. The attention was the force. It was not an attack. It was simply the King being aware of the Creator as a real thing, and the Creator could not survive that much reality.

The Creator’s body came apart. His wings did not fall. They bent backward through His own chest and re-emerged between His ribs, white and sharp and covered in His own burning blood. His halos snapped one by one and became nooses around His neck, and He clawed at them with hands that were already peeling away from the bone. His face, once a blank white flame, began to crack, and beneath the cracks there was not light, but a dark, wet meat that had never been allowed to exist. His mouth opened, and inside it were not words but a long, low wail, the sound of every prayer ever spoken discovering that it had been aimed at a thing that could not save itself. The wail rose until the walls of the chamber trembled, and then it stopped, and the Creator was no longer there.

But the King did not stop with the Creator alone. The pressure of His attention, having touched the First Author, did not withdraw. It expanded. It spread outward from the private chamber in a wave of absolute unmoving force. The palace did not break. It was erased. The city did not burn. It was compressed. The planets, the stars, the galaxies, the multiverses, the hidden dimensions, every possible timeline, every imagined and unimaginable layer of existence, all of it was taken by that wave. The entire omniverse, the true and first creation that had been shaped by the Creator, was crushed into a single point. It was not an explosion. It was not a slow destruction. It was a smooth, terrifying removal, as if all of reality had been an arrangement of dust on a table and the King had passed His hand over the table once.

The point hung in the void for a moment, dense with all that had been. Then the wave withdrew, and I found myself still standing, because His Majesty had kept me whole inside the protection of His attention. The void was empty. There was no palace. There was no world. There was only Godric, seated in a plain chair that had not existed a moment before, looking at the spot where all of creation had been.

Then He restored it. Not slowly. Not with a gesture. He simply preferred that it exist again, and it did. The palace returned. The city returned. The stars returned. Every living being that had existed before the wave was placed back in its position, whole and breathing and unaware. But the Creator was not restored. The First Author, the one who had made the first soul of Godric, was gone. There was no fragment of Him left. No shadow. No echo. His name now belongs only to the old texts.

His Majesty stood and stretched, rolling his shoulders as if He had been sitting too long. He looked at me, and the warmth had returned to His eyes.

“That was the last one, I think,” He said. “The first and the last. It is a little sad. I had hoped for a better final story. But it is what it is. Come, Osvald. Let us go down. I am hungry.”

I followed Him. I could not speak. I had seen the First Author die, and I had seen the entire omniverse destroyed and restored before I could finish a thought. I understood now that there was no one left who would ever oppose Him. Not because they were afraid. Because the very category of opposition had been closed.

Clive closed the journal. The archive was silent. His hands were cold on the cover. He had known, of course, that the last resistance had ended long ago. Every court wizard of Blake’s blood knew the broad shape of it. But he had not known the details. He had not known that the Creator Himself had hidden for aeons and then come out to die. He had not known that Godric had lowered even the weakest of His outer ways just to let Him speak. And he had not truly understood, until this moment, what it meant that the King had never needed a single one of those outer ways. The aura, the shields, the passive layers—they were not armor. They were hospitality. They kept the omniverse from dying on contact with Him.

Clive thought of the palace around him, restored after the Creator’s end. It was real. It was identical. But if he thought too long about the original that had been compressed into that small marble, or the version that had been erased before the current one was remade, the floor seemed to lose its solidity. He thought of the King, somewhere above, probably smiling at a servant or asking after someone’s family, and he understood that the smile was the smile of the only real being in all of existence. Everything else was a second draft, a restored copy, a fragile permission that could be withdrawn at any instant.

The final journal had been clear. The first and greatest challenger, the Creator of all things, had been so far beneath the weakest Year Zero Godric that He could not even register as a threat. And the Godric who sat on the throne in the year 999...9 was beyond that version by an infinite margin, and would be beyond this version by another infinite margin an instant later. The gap did not close. It had never closed. It would only widen, forever, at an accelerating rate that no mind could hold.

No one would ever oppose Him again. Not because they could not. Because opposition itself had been shown, in the final record, to be less than a whisper in a dead room. The rest of existence did not tremble because Godric was cruel. It trembled because He was kind, and patient, and warm, and because He could destroy it all with a fraction of a thought that He chose not to have. That was the true horror, and it would never end.
