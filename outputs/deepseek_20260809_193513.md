# The Archive of Absolute Futility

**Year 1,000,000**

The vault beneath the palace did not smell of dust or aged parchment. It smelled of cold stone and something sharper, something metallic that clung to the back of Kaedan's throat like a swallowed copper coin. The air was perfectly still, undisturbed by any ventilation system, preserved by wards so ancient and so layered that the space existed in a pocket of temporal isolation. Nothing decayed here. Nothing aged. The journals remained exactly as their authors had left them, century after century, millennium after millennium.

Kaedan stood at the threshold of the archive, his ceremonial robes feeling impossibly heavy against his shoulders. He was twenty-two years old, the newest court wizard of Thronegate, and the hundred-and-forty-seventh direct descendant of Blake to inherit the mantle. His father, Corin, stood beside him, one weathered hand resting firmly on Kaedan's shoulder. Corin was eighty-one years old now, his face deeply lined, his mutton chops gone completely white, but his grip remained impossibly strong. A thousandfold daily amplification across sixty-one years had made his flesh into something that could crack planetary crusts.

"Every court wizard since Blake has written in these journals," Corin said, his voice a low rumble that echoed strangely in the dead air. "Reading them is not optional. It is mandatory. And it will break something inside you."

Kaedan swallowed hard. "Father, I have trained my entire life. I know the King's power is absolute. I am not a child who needs to be coddled with warnings."

Corin turned to look at him, and Kaedan saw something in his father's eyes that made his stomach clench. It was not anger. It was pity. The deep, aching pity of a man who knew exactly what was about to happen and could do nothing to prevent it.

"You have seen Uncle Godric smile at you," Corin said quietly. "You have felt his hand on your head when you were small. You have heard him laugh at your clumsy first spells. You love him, as I love him, as every court wizard before us has loved him. And that love is real, Kaedan. It is genuine. But these journals will teach you what lies beneath that love. They will teach you the shape of the abyss."

Corin released his son's shoulder and stepped back. "I will wait outside. Read slowly. Pace yourself. Some of these wizards took years to recover from what they witnessed. You have the luxury of reading their words instead of seeing it yourself. That is the only mercy you will receive."

The heavy stone door closed with a resonant thud, and Kaedan was alone.

The archive stretched before him in a long, narrow corridor, illuminated by floating orbs of soft amber light. The walls were lined with shelves carved directly into the stone, and upon those shelves rested hundreds of thick, leather-bound volumes. Each spine bore a name, a year, and a single word denoting the stat that had been tested. Kaedan walked forward slowly, his footsteps echoing in the perfect silence, and began to read.

## Year 27,341 — Aldric — Strength

God was the creator of everything. He forged the omniverse. He spoke the first word of existence. He designed the fundamental laws of reality. He created the mortal soul of Godric Himself. He was, by any conceivable measure, the most powerful being that had ever existed. The Alpha and the Omega. The Beginning and the End. The uncaused cause of all that was and is and ever would be.

And He was nothing. Less than nothing. The youngest, weakest, most pathetically underdeveloped version of Godric—the Godric of Year Zero, fresh from stasis—could have annihilated Him without effort. The current Godric, the Godric of the endless nines, is so far beyond that version that comparison is meaningless. The gap between the Godric of Year Zero and the Godric of now is infinitely larger than the gap between the Godric of Year Zero and a single dead quark. And the Godric of Year Zero was already infinitely more powerful than God.

I cannot process this. I cannot hold it in my mind. I have tried, for decades, to grasp the scale of the King's supremacy, and every time I think I have reached the ceiling, I discover that the ceiling was merely the floor of an even higher layer of impossibility. God was erased. The creator of everything was unmade with a casual decision, and the King did not even need to use his power to do it. He simply decided. A thought. A whim. A passing impulse of divine termination.

And then he picked up the last fragment of God's essence, compressed it into a marble, and added it to his collection.

I have seen the marbles. They sit on the King's desk in his private study, two small glassy orbs that he occasionally picks up and examines. One contains the compressed remains of the Fictional Armada—every hero, every story, every narrative framework of mortal imagination. The other contains the compressed remains of God Himself—the creator of existence, the source of all meaning and purpose, the being who forged the mortal soul of the King.

They are paperweights. Souvenirs. Trinkets. The King keeps them as mementos of the last times anyone dared to oppose him.

And the restored omniverse—the reality I inhabit, the air I breathe, the existence I experience—is a copy. A perfect, flawless, indistinguishable duplicate of the original, generated by the King's will to replace what was destroyed. The original omniverse, the creation of God, the reality that was born from the first word spoken into the void—is compressed inside a marble on the King's desk.

I am not real. Not in the way I thought I was. I am a restoration. A recreation. A perfect facsimile of the Osvald who existed before God's final attack. The original Osvald was annihilated along with everything else when God's destruction caused the omniverse to unravel. The Osvald who writes these words is a copy, generated by the King's casual decision to restore what was lost.

And yet I am real. I am real because the King decided I am real. My existence is contingent upon his will, but his will is absolute, and so my existence is absolute. The distinction between original and copy is meaningless when the copy is created by a being whose power transcends the concepts of originality and duplication. The King restored everything, and his restoration is perfect, and perfection is indistinguishable from truth.

But the marble remains.

The original reality—the first creation, the divine masterpiece, the existence that was spoken into being by God Himself—is compressed into a glassy sphere the size of a child's toy, swirling with the faint, golden light of its creator's last echo. The King picks it up sometimes. He tosses it from hand to hand. He peers into its depths with that warm, fatherly curiosity.

And he smiles.

"It was a good attempt," he said to me once, holding the God-marble up to the light. "The best attempt, really. The creator of everything, challenging His own creation. There is a certain poetic symmetry to it. Almost like a story."

The marble glinted in the eternal afternoon light of the palace, its swirling depths holding the compressed essence of the being who had once been the supreme authority of all existence.

"I do not think there will be any more attempts," the King continued, setting the marble back on his desk next to its twin. "The lesson has been learned. The message has been received. Creation understands now. There is no higher authority. There is no greater power. There is no secret weakness or hidden vulnerability or divine immunity. There is only me. There has always only been me. And there will always only be me."

He turned to look at me, and his warm brown eyes held a light that was not cruel, not malicious, but simply, eternally, absolutely true.

"The omniverse is peaceful now, Clive. Perfectly, absolutely, eternally peaceful. No more opposition. No more resistance. No more desperate last stands or heroic final charges. The stories are over. The battles are done. And I can finally relax."

He leaned back in his chair, his heavily muscled frame settling into the leather with a contented sigh.

"Of course," he added, his voice carrying a note of quiet, almost wistful amusement, "I do sometimes miss the entertainment. The dramatic speeches. The banger boss battle music. The colorful visual effects. The Fictional Armada, for all their ultimate futility, at least knew how to put on a show. God was rather disappointing in that regard. No theme music at all."

He picked up the God-marble again, examining it with the fond, nostalgic affection of a man remembering a pleasant afternoon.

"But I suppose one cannot have everything. Not even me."

He set the marble down. It clinked softly against the surface of the desk, a sound that echoed in the silent study like the closing of a door. The final door. The last chapter. The end of all resistance, the conclusion of all opposition, the terminal point of any hope of ever challenging the apex predator of the omniverse.

I excused myself shortly after. I could not bear to look at the marbles any longer. I could not bear to stand in the presence of the being who had unmade God and kept the remains as a souvenir. I could not bear the weight of the knowledge that I was a copy, a restoration, a perfect duplicate of a being who had been annihilated and recreated by the casual will of the King.

And yet I serve him. I will always serve him. Because he is warm. He is kind. He is genuinely affectionate toward those he loves. He asks about my family. He remembers the names of my children and grandchildren. He offers tea and biscuits during our private meetings. He is, in every way that matters to a mortal heart, the beloved, benevolent king that every subject dreams of serving.

The warmth and the horror are the same being. The affection and the absolute supremacy are indivisible. And I, Clive, descendant of Blake, court wizard of Thronegate, will spend the rest of my existence holding both truths in my heart, trying not to let the contradiction destroy me.

The marbles sit on his desk. The original omniverse. The creator of everything. Two glassy spheres, smaller than a child's toys, containing the compressed, screaming remains of the only beings who ever dared to truly challenge Godric.

No one will ever dare again.

The archive is silent. The journals are complete. The final record has been read. And the apex predator of the omniverse sits on his throne, sipping his Earl Grey tea, waiting for an entertainment that will never come.
