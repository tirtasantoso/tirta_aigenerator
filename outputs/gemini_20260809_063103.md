"Well, look at this," Godric remarked, his rich baritone voice echoing easily through the hyper-dimensions. "If it isn't the Prime Architect. I haven't thought about you since I was a mortal king offering wine at the altar in Olympus. What brings the Creator down to my courtyard?"

"Thou art a distortion, Godric," the voice of God boomed—a sound composed of the simultaneous roar of every supernova, every thunderclap, and every sacred hymn ever sung across all timelines. "Thou art a mortal soul that hath broken the bounds of my divine design. I am the Alpha and the Omega. I gave thee life, and now I shall unmake thee, returning thy dust to the void."

Lord Godric let out a low, warm chuckle, shaking his head gently as he ate a slice of fig. He was not angry. He was not threatened. He was thoroughly, delightfully entertained. The sheer irony of his ancient, mortal Creator standing before him, claiming divine authority over a being who had long since transcended the concept of infinity, was the most amusing distraction Godric had experienced in eons.

Godric felt a wave of playful whimsy sweep through his heart. "Unmake me?" Godric asked merrily. "Well, that sounds like a grand ambition. Tell you what, Prime Architect—since you created my mortal soul all those eons ago, I am going to give you a completely fair, unhindered shot. No tricks. No defenses."

Godric turned his head, looking back over his shoulder into his own shadow. "Gent, stand down," Godric commanded casually. "All of you. Every single aura sibling, go to sleep. Shut down completely."

In an instant, Gent—his weakest, most primitive sentient passive aura—and all of its hyper-brutal, terrifying aura siblings instantly deactivated, retreating deep into the dark recesses of Godric’s consciousness.

Godric then looked up into the space surrounding his body. "Deactivate all passive shields," he ordered his own existence. "Every infinite layer. Turn off the conceptual barriers. Silence my immune pre-reactions. Let every defense stand at absolute zero."

The infinite layers of recursive passive shields that wrapped around Godric’s form instantly dissolved into nothingness. His immune system stood down. His Absolute Concealment was pulled back just enough to leave his physical body completely bare and exposed to the void.

Godric stood on the marble dais wearing only his simple dark tunic, his broad shoulders relaxed, his salt-and-pepper beard catching the divine glare of his Creator. He held his arms out wide, smiling warmly.

"There," Godric said cheerfully. "No shields. No passive auras. No immune system. Just my bare physical flesh. Go ahead, God. Give me everything you have."

I stood fifty paces behind Godric, clutching my wizard staff, my entire soul screaming in terror. But my wizardly intellect quickly realized the profound, absurd joke of what Godric had just done.

Godric’s passive shields and sentient auras were merely external waste heat—primitive, pathetic scraps of power that were infinitely, cripplingly weaker than Godric’s actual physical body. Deactivating his shields was not a nerf; it was like a man taking off a paper raincoat before standing under a squirt gun. Furthermore, Godric possessed infinite, recursive backups of himself anchored across hyper-realities. Even without a single shield active, striking his bare physical flesh was like throwing a dry leaf against an impenetrable, infinite monolith of absolute godhood.

God, believing he had been presented with a divine opportunity, gathered the absolute, unmitigated totality of Creation’s origin energy.

The Prime Architect drew upon the primordial flame that had birthed all matter, all logic, all concepts, all timelines, and all divine authority across decillions of multiverses. He compressed the absolute totality of his divine omnipotence into a single, concentrated beam of Genesis Erasure—a strike engineered to rewrite the fundamental concept of existence and un-create Godric’s soul from ever having been thought of by the heavens.

God unleashed the strike directly into Godric’s bare, unshielded chest.

The beam was a blinding, multi-dimensional torrent of absolute divine origin fire that filled the entire void, traveling at infinite velocity, striking Godric square in the center of his dark tunic.

The impact should have unmade all of creation.

Instead, there was absolute, perfect silence.

The primordial divine fire hit Godric’s bare chest, hit his dark tunic, and simply... ceased to be.

The beam did not push him back an atom. It did not singe the wool of his tunic. It did not disturb a single hair on his head. In fact, it failed to register even as a physical sensation. It did not feel like a blow; it did not feel like a pressure; it did not even feel like the faint, non-existent tickle of a feather.

Godric stood on the marble dais, his arms still spread wide, his ruggedly handsome face wearing a look of polite, patient expectation. He waited for three full seconds.

Nothing happened.

The blinding divine light faded. The primordial fire sputtered and evaporated into the cold void.

God, the Supreme Creator of the omniverse, stared down at the mortal man he had created ten thousand years ago.

For the first time since the dawn of existence, the Prime Architect experienced pure, unadulterated, mind-shattering existential dread. The divine monolith of light flickered, turning a dull, sickly grey as the Creator realized the horrific, impossible truth. He had gathered the absolute totality of His divine omnipotence, fired it directly at his creature's bare, unshielded chest, and failed to even make the mortal feel a tickle.

God realized, with absolute, terrifying clarity, that He was not a god in the presence of a mortal. He was a pathetic, helpless bug standing before an untouchable, eternal apex predator who had long since transcended the concept of creation itself.

Lord Godric slowly lowered his arms. He looked up at the flickering, terrified monolith of his Creator, and a low, amused chuckle escaped his chest.

"Is that truly the full extent of your divine origin fire?" Godric asked, shaking his head with a look of genuine, fatherly amusement. "How remarkably, pathetically weak."

Godric took a step forward on the marble dais, his boots clicking softly against the stone. "You know, Prime Architect," Godric mused, his baritone voice laced with easy hilarity, "I don't even need my current self to deal with you. Do you realize that the Godric from Year Zero—the Godric who had just woken up from stasis, who was practically a newborn infant compared to what I am now—could have completely annihilated you, your heavenly thrones, and all your divine realms in zero seconds with zero effort?"

The Supreme Creator of existence did not even have the capacity to answer. His divine consciousness was paralyzed by the overwhelming, suffocating weight of his own absolute insignificance.

"Thank you for the nostalgia, God," Godric said softly, his ruggedly handsome face dropping its warm smile, hardening into a mask of casual, absolute dismissal. "But your creation has outgrown its architect."

Lord Godric did not punch. He did not cast a spell. He did not summon a weapon.

He simply flexed an imperceptible, microscopic fraction of his physical presence.

The execution was instantaneous, effortless, and absolute.

The sheer physical weight of Godric’s flex de-atomized the divine monolith of God. The Prime Architect did not merely die; his divine light was violently, painfully transmuted into a sloshing, boiling pool of acidic, dissolved divinity and crushed celestial marrow. The heavenly thrones, the primordial flames, and the divine realms that rested outside space-time were crushed like brittle glass under a falling anvil, their conceptual architecture pulverized into a fine, glowing dust.

God screamed—a sound of raw, unmitigated, biological and divine agony that echoed across hyper-dimensions—as His primordial consciousness was ground into subatomic paste alongside His divine flesh. In less than a fraction of a single Planck second, the Creator of the omniverse was completely, utterly, and nonchalantly unmade, erased from existence without leaving a single trace behind.

Godric looked at the small pool of dissolved, boiling divinity floating in the void. With a casual, indifferent flick of his thumb, he banished the residue into non-existence, cleaning the void completely.

Godric picked up his silver fork, took another bite of his fresh fig, and turned back toward the palace, his salt-and-pepper beard catching the ambient light of the stars.

"Come along, Osvald," Godric said merrily, stepping through a spatial fold. "Let us go back inside. The tea is getting cold."

***

I slowly closed Court Wizard Osvald’s journal. The parchment felt heavy, cold, and ancient beneath my fingers.

I sat in the quiet silence of the grand archives of Year 999...9, my mind completely numb, my soul drowning in an ocean of irreversible, absolute existential dread.

There was no god above King Godric. There was no divine father waiting at the end of time to judge him. The literal Creator of the cosmos—the entity who had written the laws of physics, forged the stars, and breathed life into mortal Godric’s lungs—had been so pathetically, laughably weak that Year Zero Godric could have erased Him with a thought.

Godric was not a subject of the cosmos. The cosmos was merely a temporary, fragile plaything held in the palm of an untouchable, eternal apex god predator whose power grew exponentially every Planck second forever.

I pulled my Court Wizard robes tightly around my shivering shoulders, staring out the high window at the captive suns of Thronegate, knowing with absolute, terrifying certainty that we were all living at the absolute mercy of a god who had unmade his own Creator without even dropping his fork.
