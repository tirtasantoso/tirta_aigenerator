And he sealed the pre-stasis past with an absolute, irrevocable decree: the mortal past must remain exactly as it fell."

Kaedan felt his hands trembling where they rested on the armrests. "You... you knew this at Year Zero? But if you were already omniscient, if you already possessed infinite strength..."

"I was freshly awakened, Kaedan," Godric said softly, his salt-and-pepper beard twitching with a faint, self-deprecating smile. "I was freshly crowned King of Thronegate. I had all the power in creation, but my mortal heart was still raw, bleeding, and shattered by the memory of Isabel’s screams and Reina’s blood on the marble. I possessed absolute omniscience, which meant I knew rationally that my future self had sealed the past. But grief does not care for logic. I thought to myself: I am omnipotent. I can shatter galaxies with a glance. Surely, my will is absolute. Surely, I can force my way through time, tear the sky of ancient Olympus open, catch the falling stones, incinerate the Thronegate invaders, and hold my family in my arms again."

Godric took a slow sip of his tea, his eyes looking straight through Kaedan into an unfathomable abyss of memory. "So, in Year Zero, I tried. I gathered my intent, I stepped out of linear time, and I hurled the totality of my newly awakened power backward toward the coordinate of Isabel’s wedding. I attempted to force the pre-stasis timeline to yield."

Kaedan swallowed hard, his throat clicking in the quiet room. "What happened?"

"Nothing," Godric replied simply. "I did not even make contact with the timeline. I collided with the will of my far-future self. And it was not even an active defense. My future self did not manifest a physical avatar; he did not deploy an apocalyptic superpower; he did not even turn his head to look at me. I was completely, utterly, and crushingly defeated by the mere shadow of an afterthought of his passive intent. The force holding the pre-stasis lock was so obscenely, unfathomably greater than my newly awakened Year Zero self that my entire omnipotent existence felt like a dry leaf attempting to hold back the gravitational collapse of a googolplexian black holes. I was dismissed from causality, slammed back into my throne room in Year Zero in less than a Planck second, bruised not in flesh, but in pride, fully understanding that my future self had decreed the past untouchable."

The full, terrifying architecture of the revelation crashed into Kaedan’s amplified intellect, and for the first time in his sixty-nine years of life, his mind truly fractured under the weight of the horror.

Kaedan stared at the smiling man sitting before him. Current Godric—the Godric of Year 1,000,047—was already a being of incomprehensible, infinite supremacy. For over a million years, his baseline stats had been compounding recursively by layered infinities every infinitesimal fraction of a Planck second. He had accumulated billions of apocalyptic superpowers, each one multiplying infinitely in potency, each one enhanced by the Evolved Weakness Paradox into unfair, omniverse-shattering absolutes. Current Godric was so vast, so ridiculously overpowered, that he could casually erase and reconstruct the entire omniverse an infinite number of times in zero seconds with zero effort, viewing totality as nothing more than a fragile glass bauble in his palm. The Godric of Year Zero, who had conquered Thronegate with an unmoving glance, was less than a microscopic speck of subatomic dust compared to the god sitting in this solarium right now.

And yet, that Year Zero Godric, who was already infinitely stronger than the combined totality of the omniverse, had been utterly crushed by the mere shadow of an afterthought left behind by Far-Future Godric.

What, in the name of all existence, was Far-Future Godric?

The scaling was not just broken; it was an infinite, vertical cliff of absolute absurdity. If Current Godric, with a million years of recursive, compounding godhood, was still growing toward that distant era of Year 999...9, then the scale of Far-Future Godric was something so obscenely, monstrously omnipotent that the concept of power itself became a meaningless, infantile joke. Far-Future Godric existed at a tier so astronomically detached from creation that all of reality, all multiverses, all timelines, and all iterations of past Godrics combined into a single point were less than the heat radiated from a dead skin cell.

And the crowning, sickening realization that made Kaedan’s stomach violently heave was the sheer, grotesque unfairness of what Godric had just called his "weakness."

It was not a weakness at all. It was the ultimate, impenetrable seal of his absolute invulnerability.

To any enemy, to any cosmic entity, to any hyper-dimensional resistance that had ever existed or would ever exist, the knowledge that Godric had a limitation was a cruel, mocking illusion. An enemy could never exploit the pre-stasis past, because the barrier protecting it was not a crack in his armor—it was an absolute wall enforced by the most powerful iteration of Godric that would ever exist. No one could travel back to kill mortal Godric before the stasis, because doing so would mean attempting to overpower the will of Far-Future Godric, a feat that was mathematically, conceptually, and ontologically impossible for all of creation combined.

Furthermore, it proved that Godric’s future was an ironclad, unshakeable absolute. There was no timeline where Godric fell. There was no battle where he was defeated. There was no strategy, no weapon, and no paradox that could ever halt his ascent, because his future self already existed at the end of time, reigning supreme, having already reached back into the primordial dark to birth his own godhood. Godric’s infinite growth was not a potential; it was a guaranteed, closed causal loop anchored by the undisputed apex predator of all eternity. None of his mechanics—not his amplification, not his superpower genesis, not his absolute concealment, not his sentient auras, and not his Evolved Weakness Paradox—could ever be denied, erased, or manipulated by any force in existence, because their architect was an entity who stood infinitely above even the god sitting in this room.

Kaedan gripped his knees, his knuckles white, his breath coming in ragged, shallow gasps. "Why... why would he do it?" Kaedan whispered, his voice trembling so violently that the words could barely hold their shape. "Why would your future self create the stasis? Why would he seal the past and force you to live through the grief of losing Isabel, Hebe, and Reina without ever being able to fix it?"

Godric smiled softly, picking up his pine block and running his thumb over the smooth, carved wood. His rugged, handsome face was completely serene, entirely free of the grief and rage that had driven his Year Zero self.

"I have had a million years to think on that, Kaedan," Godric murmured, his voice rich, warm, and utterly unbothered. "One can speculate endlessly. Perhaps my far-future self understood that without the genuine grief of Olympus, without the raw, mortal loss of Reina and my daughters, the man who awoke in the sanctuary would not have possessed the cold, unyielding resolve required to rule an empire of absolute order. Perhaps he knew that altering the mortal past would create a causal paradox that would unravel the aesthetic beauty of the timeline he desired. Or perhaps... he simply liked the outcome. Perhaps, from his vantage point of infinite supremacy, he looked across the tapestry of time, saw the tragedy that birthed him, and decided it was aesthetically complete. It does not matter. The motive is his alone, and I am entirely content with his design."

Godric looked across at Kaedan, his brown eyes crinkling with genuine, paternal affection. He reached out with his large, calloused hand and gave Kaedan’s trembling shoulder a firm, warm, and reassuring squeeze.

"You see, Kaedan, I am no longer troubled by my inability to change the past," Godric said with a soft, bright chuckle. "I have accepted it. In fact, I find it rather comforting. It is nice to know that no matter how vastly my power expands, no matter how many decillions of multiverses kneel before my throne, there is still one thing in this existence that keeps me grounded. My past is my own. It is immutable, untouchable, and forever preserved in the amber of my future self's will."

Godric stood up from his chair, brushing a stray wood shaving from his charcoal tunic, and stretched his broad shoulders with a relaxed, satisfied yawn. "It is getting late, my boy. You look pale, and your hands are shaking. Go home to your chambers and get some sleep. We have an imperial delegation from the Outer Core arriving tomorrow, and I need my Court Wizard sharp and composed."

Godric gave him one last affectionate pat on the back, turned, and strolled casually out of the solarium, his heavy boots clicking softly against the marble floor as he hummed an ancient, pastoral melody from a kingdom that had been dead for a million years.

Kaedan remained frozen in his chair, staring out through the dome into the vast, violet expanse of the subjugated omniverse. His tea had gone stone cold. The ambient quiet of the palace was deafening.

The existential dread that settled into Kaedan’s soul was no longer a fear of death, nor a horror of physical violence. It was a cold, suffocating, and absolute despair born of total comprehension. Creation was not a battlefield. It was not a grand cosmic struggle between good and evil, order and chaos, or power and resistance. Creation was a completed, unchangeable monument built entirely for the eternal amusement of a smiling, affectionate apex predator who was held in place only by the grander, more terrifying shadow of himself.

Kaedan looked down at his aged, trembling hands, felt the thousandfold rush of his own useless, insignificant magic surging through his mortal veins, and knew with absolute, mind-breaking certainty that from the dawn of time to the end of eternity, victory against King Godric was not merely impossible in practice. It was impossible in principle.
