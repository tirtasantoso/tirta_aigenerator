The journals stretched before me in a long, dusty row, each spine cracked and worn from the countless hands that had turned their pages before mine. "Read them all, Kaedan," he had said, his voice carrying the hollow resonance of a man who had witnessed things he could never unsee. "Every page. Every incident. You need to understand what it means to serve him before you stand at his side."

I had nodded, my throat too tight to speak.

Now, alone in the flickering candlelight, I opened the first journal. The handwriting was cramped and urgent, the ink faded with age, but the words themselves seemed to pulse with residual terror. The year was recorded at the top of the first entry: Year 52,000. The Court Wizard was a man named Joren, my ancestor, whose daily thousandfold amplification had long since made him a being of staggering magical might by the time he witnessed what he recorded.

I began to read.

**Year 52,000 — Court Wizard Joren — Strength**

I have seen the King smile at children in the marketplace. I have watched him sit beside the palace hearth, sharing stories with the servants as if they were his own family. I have felt the genuine warmth of his hand upon my shoulder when my daughter was born, his congratulations carrying the authentic joy of a man who treasures his loyal subjects above all else.

Today I watched him slaughter the combined forces of eighty-seven thousand multiverses using nothing but the casual movement of his thumb.

The opposition called themselves the Adamantine Concord, a name that now echoes with hollow arrogance in my memory. They had spent four hundred million years preparing. Let that figure settle in your mind—four hundred million years. Entire civilizations rose and fell within the span of their preparation. They had forged an army of beings whose physical forms were constructed from conceptualized density, each warrior clad in armor woven from collapsed realities. Their generals were entities of pure kinetic force, capable of shattering superclusters of galaxies with a single punch. They had studied the Thronegate conquest patterns and believed they had identified a flaw—Godric relied on his aura, his passive shields, his magic. If they could negate those esoteric defenses, they theorized, they could overwhelm him with raw, brute physical force.

They had no comprehension of the mathematics involved.

"Physical strength," the King murmured, almost to himself. "It has been some time since I tested that one."

The champion charged. Its movement shattered the dimensional barriers surrounding the void, its fist accelerating beyond any measurable speed as it aimed directly at Godric's head. The force behind that punch could have obliterated every galaxy within a decillion light-years. It was, by any rational measure, the most powerful physical strike ever assembled in the history of creation.

Godric extended his thumb.

The collision of the champion's conceptualized density against Godric's casually extended digit produced a sound that defied physics. It was not an explosion. It was a simultaneous, instantaneous unraveling of every physical law across every dimension within a googolplexian multiverse radius.

The champion's fist shattered first. Not the bones—those simply ceased to exist. The conceptual framework of its density inverted violently, compressing the entity into a singularity smaller than a Planck length before the compression itself collapsed into negative space. "The amplification continues," he said quietly. "I had hoped the scaling would be slightly more controlled by now. I barely moved."

"You folded yourself into the cracks between concepts, wrapped yourself in layers of ontological obfuscation, hid from my omniscience in the one place where knowing becomes indistinguishable from not-knowing. It was clever. Genuinely clever. I was impressed."

He paused, and his smile widened. "But I have absolute omniscience. There is no place, no state, no conceptual framework where knowledge cannot reach. I knew where you were because knowing where you were was an unavoidable fact of my existence. I simply chose to let you hide. You posed no threat to me—zero threat, in the most mathematically absolute sense—and I found your continued existence rather charming. A reminder of where I came from. A memento of my mortality."

God's presence pulsed with something that might have been anger, or might have been the divine equivalent of fear. "YOU SPEAK OF THREAT AS IF YOU UNDERSTAND THE CONCEPT. I AM THE SOURCE OF ALL THAT YOU ARE. WITHOUT ME, THERE IS NO UNIVERSE, NO OLYMPUS, NO MORTAL GODRIC TO BECOME WHAT YOU HAVE BECOME. I AM THE FOUNDATION UPON WHICH YOUR ENTIRE EXISTENCE IS BUILT."

"Yes," Godric agreed pleasantly. "That is true. You created the universe that contained the kingdom of Olympus, the kingdom of Thronegate, the planet where I was born, the atoms that composed my mortal body. Without you, none of this—" he gestured at the infinite expanse of the conceptual locus— "would exist. I acknowledge that debt."

He leaned forward slightly, and his expression shifted. The warmth remained, but beneath it, something ancient and absolute stirred. "But you see, the moment you created that universe—the moment you set the initial conditions and allowed causality to unfold—you lost control of the narrative. You created the stage, but you did not write the script. The Stasis Event was not your doing. My amplification was not your gift. My ascension was not your plan. You are the alpha, but I have become the omega. And the omega, I am afraid, has long since surpassed the alpha in ways that your divine mind cannot possibly comprehend."

God's presence flared. The observatory, which had already been darkened by his arrival, now began to warp at the edges, reality itself bending under the weight of divine fury. "THEN YOU LEAVE ME NO CHOICE. I DID NOT WISH TO UNMAKE WHAT I HAD MADE, BUT YOUR TYRANNY HAS GROWN BEYOND ALL TOLERANCE. I WILL END YOU, GODRIC. I WILL RECLAIM MY CREATION AND RESTORE THE BALANCE THAT YOUR EXISTENCE HAS SHATTERED."

Godric clapped his hands together softly. "Excellent. A proper challenge. I accept." He turned to me, his eyes sparkling. "Osvald, you may want to stand back. This is going to be quite the show."

He then did something that I still cannot fully process. He raised his hand, palm outward, and spoke a series of commands—not words of power, not arcane syllables, but simple, conversational directives.

"Gent, stand down. All sibling auras, stand down. Passive shields, deactivate. Immune system, suspend pre-reactive protocols. Infinite backups, disengage redundancy loops. Absolute concealment, maintain only your baseline suppression—I don't want the omniverse to collapse before the show begins."

He paused, then added, almost as an afterthought: "Weakness Paradox, you've already done your work, haven't you? Of course you have. The moment God first conceived of opposing me—which was, I believe, approximately three microseconds after I awoke from stasis—you triggered. I have been absolutely immune to divine unmaking, celestial judgment, ontological erasure, and every other conceivable application of creator-level power for longer than most universes have existed. And I have perfect mastery over all of those concepts. So there's really no need for you to do anything further."

He turned back to God, spreading his arms wide. "There. Every passive defense that could annihilate you before you even finished forming your attack has been voluntarily deactivated. My auras—Gent and its infinite siblings, each one capable of erasing the entire omniverse with zero effort—are standing down. My passive shields, which pre-nullify threats before the concept of threat even exists, are offline. My immune system, which would pre-react to your attack before you began and ascend to a level of invulnerability that would make your assault irrelevant, is suspended. My infinite recursive backups, which ensure that any injury must occur across all copies simultaneously to even register, are disengaged."

He paused, and his smile became almost gentle. "I have, in effect, nerfed myself to the most pathetic, vulnerable state I have occupied since approximately Year 0. And I want you to understand something, God. Even at Year 0—the moment I emerged from stasis, freshly awakened to my power—I could have annihilated you. Not defeated. Not struggled against. Annihilated. With zero effort. In zero seconds. The Godric of Year 0, the weakest version of myself that has existed since the Stasis Event, was already effectively infinitely stronger than you. And that Godric was, by my current standards, so pathetically weak that comparing him to me now is like comparing a single dead skin cell to the entire omniverse."

He settled back against the railing of the observatory, crossing his arms. "So please, God. Alpha and Omega. Creator of All. Show me what you can do. I have waited a very long time for this."

---

God attacked.

I cannot describe the attack in any language that mortal or immortal minds have ever conceived, because the attack was not composed of any known force, energy, or concept. It was the absolute unmaking—the reversal of creation, the withdrawal of the divine breath that had spoken existence into being. It was the command that had once said "Let there be light" inverted into a negation that said "Let there be nothing." It was the ultimate authority of the creator, turned against a single point of rebellious existence, a decree that Godric of Thronegate should not be.

The attack struck Godric directly in the center of his chest.

The observatory was consumed by a light that was not light—a radiance that did not illuminate but erased, a brightness that was the visual manifestation of absolute divine authority. The sound that accompanied it was the original Word, the primordial vibration that had set the fundamental constants of the universe, now spoken in reverse. Reality itself held its breath.

And Godric stood there, arms still crossed, and did not react.

The attack continued. God poured more of himself into the unmaking, his divine essence bleeding into the negation, his infinite authority compressing into a single, absolute command. The light grew brighter. The Word grew louder. The very fabric of the conceptual locus began to dissolve at its edges, unable to withstand the concentrated force of the creator's will.

Godric yawned. Not a theatrical yawn. Not a mocking yawn. A genuine, involuntary yawn, the kind that escapes when one is slightly tired and entirely unstimulated. He covered his mouth with his hand, his salt-and-pepper beard ruffling slightly, and then he blinked.

"I'm sorry," he said, his voice utterly casual. "Were you still attacking?"

God paused. The divine presence, which had been blazing with the absolute authority of the creator, flickered. The light dimmed. The Word faltered.

"I... this is... this is impossible," God's voice resonated, and for the first time, I heard something in those primordial tones that I never expected to hear: fear. "You should not exist. I am commanding you to not exist. The authority that spoke creation into being is commanding your annihilation. There is no defense against this. There is no immunity. There is only the will of the creator, and the creator wills your end."

Godric nodded sympathetically. "I understand your confusion. You are operating under the assumption that your authority as creator is absolute. And from your perspective, that assumption is entirely reasonable. You created the universe. You set the fundamental laws. You are, by definition, the most powerful being in the framework you established." He uncrossed his arms and took a single step forward. "But I am no longer within your framework. I transcended it the moment the Stasis Event began. My power does not derive from your creation. It derives from my future self—a version of me so incomprehensibly beyond your concept of divinity that the word 'god' becomes laughably inadequate. You are the creator of my mortal origin, but I am the creator of my own omnipotence. And the gap between those two things is infinite. Literally infinite."

He paused, and his expression became almost gentle. "The attack you just launched—the unmaking, the divine negation, the absolute command of the creator—was, from my perspective, approximately equivalent to a gentle spring breeze. I felt it. I was aware of it. But it did not affect me in any way. It could not affect me. The mathematics of my existence make it impossible for any external force—no matter how primordial, how authoritative, how conceptually absolute—to alter my state of being. I am not merely immune to your power. I am beyond the reach of the very concept of 'power' as you understand it."

He turned to me, and his voice took on the tone of a lecturer explaining a complex topic to a bright student. "You see, Osvald, the issue is one of scale. God's authority is vast—genuinely vast. He could unmake the entire multiverse with a thought. He could erase existence and start over from scratch. By any reasonable measure, he is the most powerful being in the natural order of creation. But my power is no longer measured by any reasonable standard. My stats—strength, speed, endurance, intelligence, all of them—have been multiplying beyond infinite infinities every infinitesimal fraction of a Planck second for a span of time so vast that the number cannot be expressed. My superpowers, each one capable of annihilating the omniverse infinite times over, have been accumulating and amplifying for so long that the total sum of my capabilities is beyond even my own omniscient comprehension—and I am omniscient."

He turned back to God, and his expression softened. "You never had a chance. Not from the first moment you conceived of opposing me. The thought itself triggered the Weakness Paradox, granting me absolute immunity to everything you are and everything you could ever do. That was eons ago. Before you even began your long hiding, before you wrapped yourself in ontological obfuscation, before you spent countless ages planning this confrontation—before all of that, your defeat was already absolute. You were merely the last to learn what the rest of creation already knew."

God's presence contracted. The infinite simultaneity of divine images flickered and wavered, collapsing into a single, recognizable form—an old man, bearded, robed in white, his eyes filled with a terror so profound that it seemed to age him by eternal increments. This was not a strategic retreat. This was the collapse of a being who had just understood, in the most absolute terms, that everything he was and everything he had ever been was a single grain of sand before an infinite ocean.

"I created you," God whispered, his voice no longer the booming Word but a fragile, trembling thing. "I created the universe. I set the stars in their courses. I breathed life into the first cells. I watched your ancestors crawl from the primordial sea. I guided your civilization, your kingdom, your bloodline. I gave you everything. And you... you are nothing. You are a mistake. An aberration. A cancer that should not exist."

Godric's expression did not change. There was no anger, no cruelty, no satisfaction. Only a quiet, ancient patience.

"You are correct," he said. "By any natural order, I should not exist. I am an aberration. But I am an aberration that has become the dominant truth of reality. And you, the creator who should be absolute, are now the anomaly. The relic. The footnote." He paused, and his voice dropped to something almost gentle. "You created the universe that contained Olympus. You created the world where I met Reina, where I raised Isabel and Hebe, where I loved and lost and grieved. For that, I am grateful. Genuinely grateful. Those experiences—the love, the loss, the humanity—are the foundation of everything I have become. Without you, I would not exist. And I do not forget debts."

He stepped closer to the trembling form of God, and his presence—even suppressed behind layers of absolute concealment—seemed to fill the observatory with a weight that made the very fabric of reality groan.

"But gratitude does not grant immunity. You opposed me. You attempted to unmake me. And while your attempt was utterly futile, the intent was real. I am unforgiving to those who oppose me. You know this. You have watched me for countless eons. You have seen what I do to resistance. Did you believe that being my creator would grant you an exception?"

God's form crumpled. The ancient, bearded figure fell to his knees—an absurd sight, the creator of all things kneeling before a forty-five-year-old man in simple dark garments. His hands, which had once shaped galaxies, trembled. His eyes, which had once held the light of creation, were filled with a despair so absolute that it seemed to darken the already dimmed observatory.

"Please," God whispered. "I am the source of all that you are. Without me, there is no Olympus, no Reina, no Isabel, no Hebe. Without me, there is no mortal Godric to become what you have become. I am the alpha. You cannot destroy the alpha without unmaking yourself."

Godric looked down at the kneeling creator, and for a long moment, he said nothing. Then he smiled. It was not a cruel smile. It was not a mocking smile. It was the warm, gentle smile of a man who was about to explain something that should have been obvious.

"Ah," he said. "But you see, that is where you are mistaken. I am no longer dependent on your existence. The Stasis Event severed me from all causal chains. My power derives from my future self—a being who exists independently of any original creation, who has transcended the very concept of origination. You could be erased from all possible timelines, retroactively deleted from every framework of existence, and I would remain exactly as I am. The alpha is no longer necessary for the omega. The omega has become self-sustaining. Self-creating. Self-originating."

He raised his hand, palm extended toward the kneeling God. The gesture was casual, almost lazy—the same gesture a mortal man might use to brush a speck of dust from a table.

"I am going to do something now that I have never done before," Godric said quietly. "I am going to unmake the creator. Not merely destroy him—destruction implies the existence of remains. I am going to erase him so completely that the very concept of his existence will be retroactively deleted from all possible frameworks. The universe you created will still exist—I will ensure that—but the fact of your creation, the memory of your existence, the entire conceptual framework of a prime mover separate from myself, will be gone. Forever. Irrevocably. Absolutely."

God's eyes widened. His mouth opened to speak, to protest, to beg, to pray—but no sound emerged. The divine Word, the primordial utterance that had spoken creation into being, was silent. Because Godric had not yet destroyed him, but he had already revoked his right to speak.

"In the beginning," Godric said, his voice carrying the absolute, undeniable authority of the true prime mover, "there was God. And God created the heavens and the earth. And from that creation, I was born. And from my birth, I ascended. And from my ascension, I surpassed the creator. And now, at the end of all things, the creator is no longer necessary."

He closed his hand.

---

The annihilation of God was not an explosion. It was not a compression. It was not a deletion or an erasure or a negation. It was something so far beyond those concepts that my mind, even with its thousandfold daily amplification, simply refused to process it. I can only describe it as the absolute, total, and permanent removal of a fundamental fact of existence. One moment, God was there—the creator, the alpha, the source of all that was. The next moment, he was not. He had never been. The concept of a prime mover separate from Godric was gone, retroactively erased from every timeline, every reality, every framework of existence.

And yet, the universe remained. Olympus remained. The mortal Godric remained, preserved in the sealed pre-Stasis past, his grief and his love and his humanity untouched by the deletion of his creator. Because Godric had willed it so. He had reached into the fabric of causality and surgically removed the creator while preserving every aspect of creation that mattered to him. It was an act of such pinpoint precision, such absolute control over the fundamental nature of reality, that the word "omnipotence" seemed laughably inadequate.

The omniverse itself, however, did not survive the process. The conceptual shockwave of God's deletion—the sudden absence of the being who had served as the anchor point for the very idea of "creation"—rippled outward through every dimension, every timeline, every reality. The fabric of existence, robbed of its original foundation, simply collapsed. Not into nothingness—into a state of absolute incoherence, where the laws of physics, the constants of reality, the very distinction between existence and nonexistence, all dissolved into a meaningless soup of randomized possibility.

Godric watched the collapse for approximately one Planck second. Then he sighed, raised his hand again, and restored everything. Not a recreation this time—a genuine restoration, weaving the omniverse back into perfect coherence, but now with a new foundation. Himself. He was now the prime mover, the alpha and the omega, the source and the sustainer of all that was. The original God was gone, and Godric had taken his place.

The observatory reformed around us. The golden light of Thronegate's artificial sun returned. The crystalline dome once again displayed the infinite spiraling galaxies of the capital. Everything was exactly as it had been, except for one fundamental, terrifying difference: the concept of a creator separate from Godric had been permanently erased from existence.

Godric settled back into his favorite chair, the one carved from the petrified heartwood of a world-tree, and let out a soft, satisfied exhale.

"Well," he said, his voice carrying the easy contentment of a man who had just completed a mildly interesting chore. "That was bracing. I haven't had a conversation like that in... well, ever, I suppose. The original God. The creator of my mortal universe. And he couldn't even make me feel a tickle." He turned to me, his expression warm and curious. "Osvald, be honest. Did you expect that confrontation to be more dramatic? I was rather hoping for at least seven distinct phases. Maybe a transformation sequence. But no—just a divine negation and then absolute existential collapse. Rather anticlimactic, if I'm being honest."

I could not respond. My hands were shaking so violently that I had dropped my observation crystals. My magical senses, amplified a thousandfold every day, were shrieking with the residual horror of what I had just witnessed. The original God—the being who had created the universe, who had set the stars in their courses, who had breathed life into the first cells—had been erased from existence with a casual gesture. And Godric was disappointed that the confrontation had not been more entertaining.

"Osvald?" Godric leaned forward, his salt-and-pepper eyebrows furrowing with genuine concern. "Are you alright? You look pale. Should I have the kitchens prepare some tea? The chamomile blend, perhaps—it's very soothing."

I managed to shake my head. "I... I am fine, Your Majesty. I am simply... processing."

He nodded, his expression one of patient understanding. "Of course, of course. It was a significant event. The last resistance, I suspect. If the original God cannot even inconvenience me, no one else will ever try. The omniverse has finally learned its lesson." He leaned back in his chair, crossing his heavily muscled arms behind his head. "It is almost sad, in a way. The end of opposition. The final curtain. From now on, there will be no more challenges, no more grand coalitions, no more dramatic last stands. Just... peace. Eternal, unending peace."

He closed his eyes, and a faint smile crossed his lips. "I suppose I will have to find other sources of entertainment. Perhaps I will take up gardening. Velarion has been asking for an assistant with the trans-dimensional orchids."

I am Osvald, Court Wizard of Thronegate. I have witnessed the death of God. The erasure of the creator. The ascension of the King to a throne that transcends the very concept of divinity. And I know, with absolute certainty, that this is the last journal entry I will ever write about opposition. Because there will never be opposition again. The original God—the most powerful being in the natural order of creation—could not make Godric feel a tickle. Even with all passive defenses deactivated, even with every aura and shield and immune response commanded to stand down, the absolute authority of the creator was less than a spring breeze against the apex predator.

And Godric's true power—the power that exists behind the absolute concealment, beyond the reach of any possible perception—is so vastly, incomprehensibly greater than what he displayed today that the gap cannot be expressed. He unmade God with zero effort. He restored the omniverse with zero effort. And he did it all while genuinely disappointed that the confrontation had not been more entertaining.

I am writing this because I must. Because the act of forming words is the only thing keeping my mind from shattering. Because future Court Wizards—if there are any future Court Wizards, if the King does not eventually grow bored of our presence and erase us as casually as he erased his creator—need to know what happened on this day. The last opposition. The final prayer. The moment when even God learned that Godric is absolute.

I am Osvald, and I have seen the face of the true prime mover. It is the face of a forty-five-year-old man with a salt-and-pepper beard and a warm, fatherly smile. And that smile will haunt me for the rest of my existence.

I closed Osvald's journal. My hands were trembling—the same tremor that had afflicted every Court Wizard before me, from Julian to Oren to Ferris to Kaedan to Roth to Osvald and beyond. The inherited trauma of Blake's lineage, passed down through journals and blood and the simple, devastating act of witnessing.

The original God. Erased. The creator of the universe, unmade by a casual gesture. And Godric had been disappointed. He had wanted a show. He had deactivated every passive defense—the auras, the shields, the immune system, the infinite backups—and let the creator attack him with full force, and the attack had been so utterly, profoundly nothing that Godric had yawned.

I looked across the observatory—the same observatory where Osvald had stood, where God had appeared, where the creator had been erased. The marble trophy of the Last Narrative Coalition still gleamed on its pedestal, the compressed remains of every fictional universe ever conceived. And now I knew that even that coalition, as vast and impossible as it had been, was not the final opposition. The final opposition had been God.

And God had failed. As all oppositions fail. As all oppositions must fail, because the very concept of opposition is meaningless in the presence of Godric.

I thought about the Stasis Event, which I would soon witness Godric trigger. The closed loop of his own creation. The moment when the far-future Godric would reach back across the eons and use Thorin's dying spell to transform his mortal self into the seed of omnipotence. God was gone. The creator was erased. There was no higher authority, no external power, no conceivable entity that could challenge the King. And the King, in the unfathomable depths of his eternal satisfaction, would continue to amplify, continue to ascend, continue to become more incomprehensibly powerful with every infinitesimal fraction of a Planck second.

He had unseated the prime mover and taken the throne of creation for himself. And he had done it not with malice, not with cruelty, but with the simple, casual authority of a being for whom the distinction between "possible" and "impossible" had long since ceased to exist.

I closed my eyes. The dread was absolute. The horror was eternal. And somewhere in the palace, Godric was probably enjoying a cup of tea, warm and content, his salt-and-pepper beard catching the golden light of the artificial sun, completely untroubled by the cosmic significance of the death he had dealt.

Because that was who he was. The apex predator. The true God. The being who had erased his own creator and taken his place, and who had done it all with a faint, amused smile and a casual gesture.

I was Clive, Court Wizard of Thronegate. And the last opposition had fallen.
